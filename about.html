About us

A movement
born of urgency.

Founded in 2017 at the University of the Witwatersrand, in response to the escalating crisis of Gender-Based Violence and Femicide in South Africa.

The Black Womxn Caucus (BWC) is a youth-led, feminist, and intergenerational movement working against Gender-Based Violence and Femicide (GBVF), which affects women (including LGBTQIA+, gender non-conforming people, and persons with disabilities) and children. That is why we write womxn, with an x.

BWC has grown from a university-based movement into a national one with a continental and international footprint, and has pioneered pivotal moments in shifting the GBVF discourse in South Africa, including the historic #TheTotalShutdown and #SandtonShutdown.

We focus on advocacy, education, capacity-building, research and policy development, institutional reform, and psychosocial support. We work with adolescents and youth in and out of education, and with working-class women in urban, peri-urban, informal, and rural communities.

GBVF shows up as economic, political, social, physical, geographical and psychological violence. We organize against all of it.

Main objectives

·

Use feminist popular culture and education to advocate against Gender-Based Violence and its manifestations on a micro, meso, and macro level.

·

Facilitate education and conscientization through extended dialogue and interaction with perspective, ideology, and paradigmatic framework.

·

Form a constituency with political weight, dedicated to effecting protest and championing the causes of other social movements whose mandate is in line with our own.

Secondary objectives

·

Educate and empower the most vulnerable members of our society through participatory approaches.

·

Create a growing culture of activism through the involvement of various sectors in society to respond to societal ills.

·

Help create feminist organizing platforms that develop dialogue and consensus around the manifestations of GBV and its implications on women, children, and society at large.

Mbali Mngomezulu is a training and development practitioner in the HR field, focusing on improving the skills employees need for their current roles and on developing training and succession plans. She enjoys reading, watching series and learning new things. She has a particular interest in GBV awareness and advocacy and has been active in a number of GBV programs and initiatives led by various organisations, including BWC. She is currently working on her honours degree in psychology, as well as her insurance accreditation, as she is currently an apprentice broker.

Mems Ramaila

Board member

Mems Ramaila is a Sustainability Practitioner and Research Associate at the University of Johannesburg’s Centre for Ecological Intelligence. Passionate about nature-based solutions, she has developed an innovative leadership development model inspired by African honeybees. The model is designed to empower corporates, business schools and the public sector to embrace adaptive and collaborative ways of leading.

Philanimpilo Lionel Mlawuli

Board member

Read bio

Philanimpilo Lionel Mlawuli is a businessman with over 20 years of business experience across sectors, including logistics, property development, real estate, retail, and infrastructure development. He entered the business world through the taxi industry (his family remains influential, mainly in the South of KwaZulu-Natal, with over 32 years of experience), where he honed his business skills and acumen. He is the Founder and Director of Impi Loy Group and Holdings, established in 2015. The Group has expanded its portfolio into water and sanitation, mining, and locomotive supplies, and in recent years has worked closely with municipalities and water boards to provide innovative solutions to the mounting water challenges facing South Africa and the broader region. His love for real estate led him to establish Live Life Real Estate, focusing on land acquisition, property development, and house sales. He is a passionate humanitarian, committed to community development, and currently serves as a board member of the Black Womxn Caucus.
